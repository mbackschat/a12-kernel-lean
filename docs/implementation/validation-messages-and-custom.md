#### Rule negation and `Non<Operation>` twins

- `boundary`: [`ConditionNegation.lean`](../../A12Kernel/Semantics/ConditionNegation.lean) defines De Morgan negation of a condition tree with a per-leaf complement tree. [`Negate.lean`](../../A12Kernel/Reference/Dm/Negate.lean) performs the same rewrite on the parse tree with the complement table, and [`Print.lean`](../../A12Kernel/Reference/Dm/Print.lean) prints the result as Kernel-runnable text in the model's language.
- `boundary`: the single-predicate pairs, each negating in both directions, are `FieldFilled`/`FieldNotFilled`, `GroupFilled`/`GroupNotFilled`, `AllFieldsFilled`/`NotAllFieldsFilled`, `AtLeastOneFieldFilled`/`NoFieldFilled`, `AllGroupsFilled`/`NotAllGroupsFilled`, `AtLeastOneGroupFilled`/`NoGroupFilled`, `FieldValueIncludedInValueList`/`FieldValueNotIncludedInValueList`, `AtLeastOneFieldValueIncludedInValueList`/`NoFieldValueIncludedInValueList`, `PatternMatched`/`PatternViolated`, and the comparison flips `==`/`!=`, `<`/`>=`, `<=`/`>`. A complement keeps its original's arguments and `In` list unchanged. The spec documents `@From` only on `RepetitionNotUnique`, which has no complement.
- `boundary`: `Valid` and `Invalid` are a pair only when their single argument is a `Date(...)` call. With an unregistered type name the field form `Valid(Field, "Type")` and its `Invalid` both fire on a filled field ([checkpoint](../sources/evaluation-and-application-routes.md#src-negation-pair-supplement)), and registration is no part of the condition, so the field form refuses the negation.
- `boundary`: four predicates have a compound complement over the same arguments: `FieldsNotCollectivelyFilled` negates to `(AllFieldsFilled Or NoFieldFilled)`, `GroupsNotCollectivelyFilled` to `(AllGroupsFilled Or NoGroupFilled)`, `MoreThanOneFieldFilled` to `NumberOfFilledFields <= 1`, and `NotExactlyOneFieldFilled` to `NumberOfFilledFields == 1`. Every other predicate, the tolerance comparisons and `CustomCondition` refuse the negation, named by their keyword spelling, a tolerance comparison by its operator; when several leaves refuse, the leftmost names the refusal.
- `boundary`: [`NonOperation.lean`](../../A12Kernel/Reference/Dm/NonOperation.lean) reads the `Non<Operation>` extension through `parseNonText`, where `NonOp(args)` is spelled `Non` and `Op`'s spelling in the model's language, and lowers each twin to the negation of `Op(args)`. A twin exists exactly where a call-form predicate has a complement; comparisons and the pattern operators, which already have complement operators, and `CustomCondition` have none. A twin is read only as a call: a quoted name, a name in brackets and a bare argument are never twins, and in the Kernel language `NonFieldFilled` stays a name.
- `recursive-rewrite`: `lowerNon` visits reference filters, recursive index keys and `@From` scopes while retaining each attachment owner. [Rewrite locks](../../A12Kernel/Reference/Dm/NegateLocks.lean) compare the resulting trees with parsed ordinary twins; [raw syntax support](cross-clause.md#cap-semantic-index-admission) does not imply those references are statically admitted.
- `boundary`: static legality is not part of the rewrite. Negating an iteration guard produces a rule the Kernel refuses with `MVK_NEG_CONDITION_IN_ITERATION` and Lean's rule assembly with `negativeConditionInIteration` ([checkpoint](../sources/evaluation-and-application-routes.md#src-negated-corpus)).
- `assurance`: P in [`Proofs/ConditionNegation.lean`](../../A12Kernel/Proofs/ConditionNegation.lean): over the total evaluator `evalVerdict`, `negate_exclusive` shows a tree never fires with its negation when none of its leaves fires with its complement, and `negate_exhaustive` that one of them fires when every leaf is decided.
- `assurance`: P in [`Proofs/NegationPairs.lean`](../../A12Kernel/Proofs/NegationPairs.lean), each pair's regions over its shared evaluator: the numeric comparison flips, scalar equality, direct String equality (also silent on an empty literal), field and group presence, and the field and group quantifiers; a not-collectively predicate excludes both members of its complement. The pattern pair's facts are the existing `stringPattern_evalResolved_value_complement` and its empty and unknown neighbours.
- `assurance`: E in [`NegateLocks.lean`](../../A12Kernel/Reference/Dm/NegateLocks.lean): the sixteen unguarded grid originals negate to the complements the Kernel ran, a list and scope survive, the date and field forms of `Valid` split as stated, the surface comparison complement is the semantic one, printing reads back to the same tree in both languages, and the twins lower as stated.
- `assurance`: L over the [pair grid](../sources/evaluation-and-application-routes.md#src-negation-pair-grid), its [supplement](../sources/evaluation-and-application-routes.md#src-negation-pair-supplement) and the [negated corpus](../sources/evaluation-and-application-routes.md#src-negated-corpus), full and partial validation alike: no listed pair fires both members on the Kernel, German spellings fire as English ones, and the population replay, which compares full-validation messages only, matches the Kernel on every negated rule it represents. The value-list, `Valid`/`Invalid` date, `MoreThanOneFieldFilled` and `NotExactlyOneFieldFilled` pairs rest on that evidence and have no pair theorem.
- `non-law`: polarity is per pair: `AllFieldsFilled` fires VALUE where `NotAllFieldsFilled` fires OMISSION. A negation may also fire on an instance without content, where its original cannot: `FieldNotFilled(A)` on the grid's `blank` document.

### §13 — message interpolation

<a id="cap-whole-rule-authoring-and-rendering"></a>
#### Whole-rule authoring and rendering

- `boundary`: Checked nonempty flat rule templates admit text, `$$`, `$Field$`, and `$Field.value$`. A parameter's entity spec is the **shared path grammar** the condition parser uses, decoded into the one existing `SurfaceFieldPath` and resolved by the one existing field resolver rather than a second lookup: a bare name, a group-qualified relative path, an absolute path, and a parent walk with an optional explicit turning point all reach a nonrepeatable field, which must be referenced by the condition before direct lowering to the one-pass post-fire renderer. The value suffix is taken at the end of the whole spec, which the grammar itself settles rather than this fragment choosing: the value terminal is not one of the four a name may end with, so a trailing one is always the suffix.
- `boundary`: A name colliding with a terminal is written in the grammar's single-quote escape, erased before lookup so an unnecessary quote is transparent, and the collision is checked at any path level naming the offending segment; this producer's quoting requirement is deliberately narrower than the condition language's, because a caller-supplied set of terminals is historically accepted unquoted inside an entity name. A field reference may instead carry an Enumeration **category** suffix, whose three gates are the Kernel's own in its order — a missing name, a non-Enumeration field, and an undeclared category name reached through the one existing Enumeration projection boundary — and which carries neither the value suffix's non-starred nor value-validation gate, while ordinary condition membership still applies.
- `boundary`: The checked category access renders through the declaration's category mapping. Enumeration `.value` rendering remains caller-supplied in this fragment, with the canonical display contract selecting the declared value label and falling back to the stored token. A reference may finally carry a **semantic-index key**, `For "k"` or `For SomeField`, decoded in last position after any other suffix, with a quoted literal and a keying-field path as its only two spellings and a nested key refused; because a semantic index needs a repeatable group, this nonrepeatable fragment decodes such a parameter and then refuses it at its own boundary rather than borrowing the Kernel's pairing class, and the measured gate it cannot pose is on the **index** rather than on the field.
- `boundary`: One **non-field** form is admitted: the Base Year terminal with an optional signed offset, gated only on the model declaring a Base Year and resolved at authoring because it depends on the model alone. The selected language's spellings are data rather than built in, since the parameter grammar is bilingual and only a handful of terminals differ. A doubled category arrow retains its first suffix long enough for the Kernel's ordered kind and membership gates, so its class depends on that suffix; other unsupported shapes in this bounded fragment remain parse failures. The certificate retains the authored spelling beside its decoded path, so a diagnostic and an Explain consumer can quote what was written
- `boundary`: A second **position** is admitted beside the name one: `$#…$` takes a group, spelled as an absolute path, a path relative to the rule's group with the name grammar's parent walk and turning point, or one of two keyword shorthands for the endpoints of the rule's own ancestor chain, `RootGroup` naming that chain's root rather than a model-wide one. The named group must be **available**: an ancestor of the rule's group or its own, or a group holding a field the condition reads. An absolute path resolves in the two gates the Kernel reports apart, a declared root first and availability second; a relative one refuses a walk past the root and a wrong turning point on classes of their own. Availability needs no separate existence test, because every ancestor of a declared group and every group holding a declared field is itself declared.
- `boundary`: **the value suffix is caller-supplied too, and had been the one exception.** It was matched as the literal `.value` while every sibling terminal was profile data, so a German-language profile would have parsed with the English word; it is now `valueSuffixTerminal`. Each language is locked on both sides of the outcome, and the other language's word fails as a **parse** — its residue is no legal entity spec — rather than at the unquoted-terminal gate. Which word a given Kernel model takes stays the caller's data; the Kernel's terminal tables give `value` and `Wert`, read from the tables rather than measured on a German model.
- `boundary`: Group segments take the name grammar's quote escape, and only an unquoted word opens a keyword, so quoting a terminal turns it back into an ordinary group name. A dotted continuation joins its name segment. A malformed spelling is a parse failure on the Kernel's own **three** classes rather than one: a lexer fault, a trailing separator, and a doubled separator are separate diagnostics, decided by the malformed shape,.
- `boundary`: Each of those classes answers before the lookup, in the order the parser meets them, while a token left after a path answers only once the path resolves to an available group, and a bare word that names nothing below the rule's group falls back to a field short-name lookup with its own ambiguity class. The character position the Kernel's lexer code additionally carries is a payload this fragment does not reproduce. Two historic terminals keep the Kernel's own distinct refusal class.
