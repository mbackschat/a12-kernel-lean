- `model`: the [flat diamond](#src-diamond-dependency-clear-kinds) moved inside a repeatable group — `Rows` with `max 5`, holding `Base`, `Alt`, `Gate`, `Err` and the same four computed targets `P` (under `FieldFilled(Gate)`), `A = [P]`, `B = [Alt]`, `Final = [A] + [B]`. Every computation is declared in the repeatable group, so the whole diamond runs once per row.
- `question`: the flat round settled ordering and the two clear-kinds on a nonrepeatable root, and named the repeatable crossing as its own residue. A settled mechanism compiles wherever its types fit, so crossing that boundary is a claim rather than an inheritance under the [carrier rule](../../CLAUDE.md) — and this boundary is one where the two arms are already known to differ elsewhere.
- `claim`: **all three producer states coexist in one call, each confined to its own row, and each behaves exactly as it did flat.** One document, three rows: a clean row answers `P = 7, A = 7, B = 5, Final = 12`; a row whose `Base` is malformed clears `P`, `A`, and `Final` while its `B` still answers `5`; and a row whose `Gate` is absent clears `P` silently, has `A` read it as **empty** and answer `0`, and `Final` answer `5`.
- `one-call-is-the-separator`: this is stronger than running the three states as three documents, and the strength is the point. If the plan were scheduled once for all rows rather than per row, the malformed row's poison would have to either reach the clean and precondition rows or become indistinguishable from the precondition row's clear. Neither happens: the clean row computes in full and the two clear-kinds stay apart **inside the same call**, on the same model, at the same instant.
- `disposition-per-row`: with stale values stored in every target, the malformed row's `P`, `A`, and `Final` come back `cleared: true` while the precondition row's `P` alone clears and its `A` still answers `0`. So the clear-then-read-empty order holds per row, and the aborted targets' disposition is again visible only where there was something to clear.
- `addresses-are-row-exact`: `formalErrorsInOperands` names `Rep/Rows[2]/Base` and nothing else, so the eager operand inventory is row-addressed rather than field-addressed, and a consumer can locate the offending instance from it.
- `local-consequence`: none. The account already schedules per instantiated target row and routes each producer state through the same `dependencyObservation` split, so this crossing needed evidence rather than code. No new conformance case: the branches are already represented, and a per-row fixture would exercise the same two functions the flat cases cover.
- `limit`: one repetition level, three rows, Number targets only, one join of arity two, `validateFull` plus `compute` on one document pair. Two levels, branches at different levels, a join across rows and over-limit rows are measured at the [two-level checkpoint](#src-two-level-repeatable-join).
- `sync`: locally originated, and it **confirms** the flat account and the existing per-instance confinement rule on a new carrier rather than correcting either, so it opens no ledger entry and dispositions none.

#### A four-deep dependency diamond orders correctly, and the two clear-kinds part ways at a plain operand read, measured locally 2026-09-03

- `revision`: one `model new`, one `apply` of eight fields, four `computation add` and one `computation modify`, then two `:adapter:kernelProbe` runs with `observe: ["validateFull", "compute"]` over seven documents. a12-dmkits `acced5d6012b15c0c3145d2a236e61bf2ad74246` on `main` with `source.state: CLEAN`, `dmtool` 0.13.0, Kernel `30.8.1` built and runtime, both codegen strategies with `enginesAgree: true` on every row; `model check` valid with zero diagnostics and every authoring child `KERNEL_CONFIRMED`. Sibling `git status --short` empty before and after.
- `model`: a nonrepeatable root with Number `Base`, `Alt`, `Gate`, `Err` and four computed targets — `P = [Base]`, `A = [P]`, `B = [Alt]`, `Final = [A] + [B]`. That is a **diamond** four levels deep: one producer, two independent dependents, one join. The [reverse-authored triple](#src-scalar-mixed-reverse-authored-triple) is a line, and its `limit` names wider plans and generic scheduling as unmeasured; a line cannot show whether a join is ordered after both branches or whether a failure on one branch reaches it.
- `ordering-claim`: **the order is correct at depth four through the join.** On `Base = 7, Alt = 5` the outcomes are `P = 7`, `A = 7`, `B = 5`, `Final = 12`. Two wrong orders are refuted by that single row: evaluating the join before its branches gives `Final` nothing to read, and evaluating the branches before the producer gives `A = 0` and `Final = 5`. The `Final = 12` cell requires the whole chain in order.
- `claim`: **the two clear-kinds are not one state — a silently cleared producer reads EMPTY while an errored one poisons its reader, and the difference is visible only downstream.** With `Base` malformed, `P`, `A`, and `Final` all clear while `B` still computes `5`. With `P` instead cleared by an **unmet precondition** — no malformed cell anywhere in the document — `A` reads it as empty and answers `0`, and `Final` answers `5`. Same topology, same `Alt`, and only the *kind* of the producer's clearing differs.
- `the-artifact-cannot-tell-them-apart`: in both cases the producer's own outcome reads `cleared: true, errored: false`, and the precondition case reports nothing at all in `formalErrorsInOperands`. So the per-outcome flags collapse a distinction the Kernel plainly makes, and the **only** channel that separates them is the dependents' own outcomes. A consumer reading `errored` to predict propagation is wrong on the malformed row. This is a route fidelity limit rather than a semantic finding, and it is instrument feedback for a12-dmkits rather than a ledger entry.
- `poison-crosses-the-join`: `Final` reads one poisoned operand and one clean one and **clears**, rather than substituting empty-zero for the poisoned side. The absent-`Base` row is the control that makes this informative: absence *does* substitute, giving `P = 0`, `A = 0`, `Final = 5`. So empty substitution and poison are both live on this exact join, selected by the producer's state and not by the join's shape.
- `disposition-needs-something-to-clear`: with nothing stored in the targets the aborted ones are simply **absent** from the outcome list; with stale values stored they come back `cleared: true`. Both batches carry that pair, so the earlier [aborted-target row](../sources/having-filter-probes.md#src-nested-correlated-filter-and-poison-scope) is reproduced on a dependency chain rather than a filtered aggregate.
- `bare-constant-under-a-precondition`: the same model closes a separate open item on a third batch. Re-expressing one branch as the bare constant `42` under `FieldFilled(Gate2)` is admitted, and it writes `42` when the precondition is met — so a constant operation honours a precondition exactly as a computed one does. Unmet, the branch clears and the join reads it as **empty**, answering `7` rather than `49`, which reproduces the silent-clear rule on a producer kind the earlier rows did not cover.
- `the-clear-is-visible-within-the-call`: the sharpest row of the three. With `99` **stored** in that branch and the precondition unmet, the join answers `7` — not `106`. So the dependent does not read the stale stored value: the clear takes effect before the read, inside the same call. A reimplementation that cleared at application time and let dependents read pre-call storage produces `106` here, and every fresh-value row of the [reverse-authored triple](#src-scalar-mixed-reverse-authored-triple) still passes for it, because that round propagated new *values* rather than a clear.
- `not-a-call-wide-abort`: `B` computes its value in **every** failing row of both batches. One call therefore produced one value beside three clears, which is what makes the clearing a per-target propagation rather than a plan-level abort.
- `local-consequence`: no clause or code change, and the account was already exactly this. `NumericTargetOutcome.dependencyObservation` maps `.noValue` to `.empty` and `.rejected`/`.invalidNoValue`/`.inheritedPoison` to `.poisoned`, and [`Conformance/NumericDependency.lean`](../../A12Kernel/Conformance/NumericDependency.lean) already asserts every arm of that split. What this round adds is the **external** evidence it lacked, on a channel that had none. It also supplies the missing witness for [SG4](../SEMANTICS-GAPS.md#sg4--computation-scheduling-and-state-transition)'s standing prohibition on using deltas as the dependency overlay: `NumericDelta` deliberately collapses clean no-result, domain invalidity, and inherited poison into one `.cleared`, exactly the collapse the Kernel's dependents refuse.
- `no-retained-bytes`: deliberate, not an omission. The reading is what carries value here and the mechanism it confirms is already locked internally, so a retained artifact would need a Lean projection consumer to earn its place under the [compact pipeline](../SEMANTIC-CAPSULE-PIPELINE-PROPOSAL.md) — Lean code for one reading, guarding a branch that already has cases. The model and documents are reproducible from the `model` row above.
- `limit`: one nonrepeatable root, Number targets only, one join of arity two, copy, addition, and one bare constant, `en_US`. Not measured here: whether any instrument channel exposes the ERRORED-versus-cleared distinction directly. A join whose branches poison from different producers, three operands and String joins are measured at the [join checkpoint](#src-join-poison-and-substitution), and the repeatable crossing at the [repeatable diamond](#src-repeatable-diamond-per-row-states).
- `sync`: locally originated. The clear-kind divergence at a plain operand read and the join's poison crossing are additions to [`spec/09`](../../spec/09-computations.md) and go outbound as `SPEC-2026-09-03-09`; the ordering, absent-substitution, and not-a-call-wide-abort rows **confirm** existing clauses and add no request.
